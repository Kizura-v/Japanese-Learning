# Japanese Learning

Practical Japanese for **natural conversation, sentence construction, family life, parenting, cooking, greenhouse work, travel, and IT/cybersecurity**.

## Start here

- **[Daily Training — E-R-K](daily-training/README.md):** 15-minute routine, a 14-day progression, sentence-order practice, particles, conjugation, natural casual speech, and realistic dialogues.
- **[Master Japanese Study Dataset](data/japanese-study-master.csv):** 961 entries exported from the connected Google Sheets workbook across 18 subject tabs (snapshot on 2026-10-27).
- **[Google Sheets — Everyday Study Workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1d9_zZAU9MBIifsuHmX5HsnhGb4KQBnjrIFp7xTV9KDk/edit):** primary mobile-friendly study interface.

## Study conventions

**E-R-K** in daily drills means **English | Romanji | Kana** (phonetic Japanese, using hiragana and katakana as appropriate), with a fourth **Japanese** reference column. This keeps natural Japanese writing rather than transcribing every sentence entirely into katakana.

The source workbook has exactly eight columns in each study tab:

`Eng | Romanji | Hiragana | Japanese | Examples | Group | Usage | Register`

The repository CSV adds `Tab` as its first column to preserve subject identity in one file. Section headings and spreadsheet colors are not represented in this flat export; use Google Sheets for the visual organization and yellow priority highlights.

## How to study

1. Open [Daily Training](daily-training/README.md).
2. Say the English prompt aloud, then produce the Japanese without looking.
3. Check Romanji, Kana, and the Japanese reference.
4. Change tense, particle, or level of politeness and repeat.
5. Track missed phrases and review them the next day.

## Data maintenance

Google Sheets is the live source; `data/japanese-study-master.csv` is a **point-in-time snapshot**, not an automatic sync. Export again after major workbook edits. Review translations, readings, and dialect labels before promoting them into teaching materials. Preserve the workbook's eight-field structure and avoid removing useful near-synonyms whose usage differs.
